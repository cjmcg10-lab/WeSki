# Forecast history, observations and archive (server side of issue #5)

Part of #1. The engine side (how history changes scores and confidence) is in `docs/FORECAST_SCORING.md`. This page covers what the Worker keeps and serves.

## What is kept, and when

A resort is only recorded when somebody asks about it with `&id=<resort id>` on `/api/weather` (`loadWeather` in `src/weather.js` adds it for any place that has an id), and only when the id is on the server-side resort list: `public/forecast/catalog-index.json` (4,792 resorts, `idAliases` map old ids to current ones), read through the Worker's `ASSETS` binding and kept in memory for an hour and in the data centre's cache (`worker/catalog.js`). Resorts whose catalog row has no verified height are not on the usable list. The place stored for the resort (points, heights, time zone) is the list's, never the query string's, and a request is recorded (and answered from the stale copy) only when its own place is exactly the listed one: the same two points (the numbers the app sends are the list's own), heights equal to the list's rounded to metres, and the same time zone. A point a kilometre away or a height 100 m off records nothing, so a nearby place's forecast cannot be stored as the resort's history. Anything else, an invented id or another place under a real id, creates no store and writes nothing. In the dev config (`wrangler.jsonc`, no `ASSETS` binding) nothing is recorded; the deploy config (`wrangler.assets.jsonc`) has the binding. Nothing crawls resorts. A new Durable Object class, `ForecastHistory` (one per resort id, SQLite backed, binding `FORECASTHIST`, migration tag `v3` in both wrangler configs), holds:

| Key | What | Size (measured with the recorded Stowe forecast) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `hist` | The last 72 hourly rows for the summit and base, plus `pending` (the forecast hours still ahead, see below): temperature, feels-like, snowfall (cm in that hour), wind, gusts, visibility, weather code, plus `issued` (when the forecast was issued), `acc` (the hours the source's snowfall figure covered: 1 to 6 for NWS, 1, 6 or 12 for MET Norway), `src` (`nws`, `met` or `nws-obs`) and `model` (`nws_ndfd`, `met_locationforecast` or `obs:<station>`) | about 22 KB when full (about 157 bytes a row), plus up to about 15 KB of pending hours outside the USA |
| `last` | The last good `/api/weather` response, for the stale fallback | about 21 KB |
| `a:<issue time in ms>` | One archived forecast (see Archive) | about 9.5 KB each |
| `meta` | The place first seen, the newest issue time, the last archive time, `recordedAt` (the 30 minute gate) and `histHash` (a hash of the stored `hist`) | about 0.2 KB |

Hours a source did not actually cover are never recorded: both adapters mark them `filled` (NWS: before or between its series, MET Norway: before its first entry, which a copy of the first temperature and a sixth of the next 6 hours of precipitation would otherwise stand in for) and `forecastRows` skips them, so they cannot invent snowfall or overwrite an earlier real forecast row. NWS `issuedAt` is the newer of the grid's `updateTime` and `generatedAt`. That issue time is a loose age signal: NWS grids go 6 to 12 hours between updates, so the engine (1.3.1, `docs/FORECAST_SCORING.md`) treats the phone copy's age (`fetchAgeHours`, 6 h or more is Low) and the server's `stale` flag as the real problems, and the model's issue age only loosely (Medium from 18 h, Low from 30 h).

**Pending hours (MET Norway, outside the USA).** MET's answer starts at the current hour, so no finished hour would ever be recorded. Each recording therefore also keeps the forecast hours still ahead (the next 48, summit and base) in `hist.pending`, keyed by UTC hour and carrying the issue time and source. A newer issue replaces an older one for the same hour. Once an hour has passed, its newest pending value is promoted to the history as a forecast, marked `observed: false` (history rows never carry `observed: true`; "measured" is still `src: nws-obs` without `snowEstimated`). A promoted hour gives way to any later real row for that hour (a forecast row from the series itself, or an observation, whatever its issue time), and never replaces one. Hours a source marked `filled` are never pending, so nothing invented is promoted. NWS needs none of this: its series starts at local midnight, so its completed hours are recorded directly. A MET series recorded at 09:00 and again at 15:00 leaves 09:00 to 14:00 in the history with the 09:00 issue's values; the 15:00 issue's values take over the hours it covers once they pass. Promoted hours count as history hours for confidence but never as measured hours, so High confidence outside the USA still waits for a second source.

Rows are keyed by the hour's UTC start, so daylight-saving changes cannot merge or split hours: a fall-back day keeps 25 rows and `GET /api/history` labels the repeated hour `01:00` twice, in order; a spring-forward day has 23 rows and no `02:00`. Local labels are made when the rows are read, from the resort's time zone.

**Overlap.** A row holds one hour's snowfall, so two forecasts that cover the same hour never add up: the later issue replaces the earlier one for that hour, an earlier issue arriving late is ignored, and a measured (observed) hour is never replaced by a forecast. NWS gives snowfall in blocks of up to 6 hours; the adapter spreads a block evenly over its hours and records the block length in `acc`. NWS observations that report the last 1, 3 or 6 hours of precipitation are claimed finest first: a longer total only spreads what the shorter ones left over, so overlapping totals are never counted twice. Most stations report no total at all; the hour then keeps the forecast's snowfall (flagged `snowEstimated`) until a later forecast confirms it, and the hour does not count as measured (`observed` is false and it is left out of `observedHours`), so High confidence needs real snowfall measurements, not only a station's temperature and wind.

## NWS observations (USA only)

For each of the summit and the base, the Worker reads `points/<lat>,<lon>` (already cached for the forecast), the grid's station list (24 hour cache), picks the nearest station within **25 km** whose height is within **300 m** of that site (distance plus 1 km per 50 m of height gap decides between candidates), and reads its last 72 hours of observations (`start` rounded to the hour, 15 minute cache, 500 at most). Requests use the User-Agent `weski-build` and go through the same Cloudflare cache as the forecast, so a busy resort costs the NWS at most one request per cache window. Most summits have no station that close in height, so usually only the base has measured hours. A failed observation fetch is logged and skipped; the forecast and the recording carry on.

## Endpoints

- `GET /api/weather?...&id=<resort id>`: unchanged shape, with new top-level fields: `model`, `issuedAt`, `fetchedAt`, `ageHours` (hours since the source issued it), `stale` (false), `resolutionKm` (nominal model grid: 2.5 for the US grid and MET Norway's Nordic model, 9 for MET Norway's global model elsewhere), `gridElevation` (`{ summit, base }` in metres from the NWS grid cell; null for MET Norway), `family` (the model family behind the answering source) and `alerts` (winter storm, snow, high wind and wind chill alerts in force or announced, see `docs/FORECAST_SOURCES.md`). The `id` also switches on recording.
- **Stale fallback, only on request.** With `&id=<listed id>&stale=1` (only the Forecast screens send `stale=1`; the existing weather sheet does not, so it never shows an old forecast as current), if no source answers and the resort has a recorded forecast issued within the last 48 hours, the same endpoint returns that forecast with `stale: true` and `ageHours` counted from its issue time (cache 60 seconds), and only for the listed place. The engine reads those two fields and gives Low confidence (`CR_STALE`). In every other case the answer is the old 502. `loadWeather` never stores a stale answer in the phone cache.
- `GET /api/history/<resort id>`: `{ id, tz, sources, ageHours, coverage: { summit: { hours, observedHours }, base: {...} }, summit: { hourly }, base: { hourly } }`. Each `hourly` has the same fields as the weather's (`time` local labels, `temperature_2m`, `apparent_temperature`, `snowfall`, `wind_speed_10m`, `wind_gusts_10m`, `visibility`, `weather_code`) plus `observed` (a boolean per hour). Hours run oldest first up to the last completed hour. 404 for an id not on the list, and until the listed resort has been recorded (an id off the list never touches a store). Pass it to the engine as `opts.history`: the Forecast screens fetch it (`loadHistory` in `src/weather.js`) after the first draw, rescore when it arrives, keep it on the phone for 20 minutes like the weather, and skip it offline and for ids starting with `pin-`. The calendar's "Snow history is limited for now" line shows only while some listed mountain has fewer than 48 history hours. `ageHours` is the age of the newest source that fed the history. A resort that has just started being watched only has the completed hours of today (from local midnight) plus, in the USA, up to 72 hours of observations; the rest builds up by itself.
- The archive has no public route yet (phase 8 adds one, or reads `ForecastHistory.archive(sinceMs, limit)` from inside the Worker).

Recording is cheap by design: the id and place are checked first, then a cheap pre-filter in Cloudflare's cache (30 minutes, per data centre, claimed only after a recording succeeds) skips repeat requests, and the real gate is inside the store (`meta.recordedAt`: a second `record` within 30 minutes is refused, whichever data centre sent it). The recording runs after the response is sent (`ctx.waitUntil`), and nothing is written when nothing changed. Before it fetches NWS observations the Worker asks the store whether the 30 minute gate is open (`due`, one small read), and skips the fetch and the recording when it is closed.

**Write skipping.** A recording builds the next `hist` (rows and pending hours), hashes it and compares the hash with `meta.histHash`; `hist` is written only when it differs. `last` is written only for a newer issue whose hourly numbers changed (NWS stamps a new issue time on almost every answer; the stamp alone writes nothing, only `meta`), and an archive entry only every 6 hours. `meta` is written by every recording that passes the gate (the place, issue time, `recordedAt`), so the 30 minute gate closes reliably after any recording that reached the store, and a change of place is saved.

## Archive

Every forecast is archived as issued, at most one per resort every 6 hours (4 a day), and pruned after 28 days. An entry holds: `v` (`SCORING_VERSION` that scored it), `issued`, `fetched`, `source`, `model`, `resolutionKm`, `ms0` (UTC milliseconds of the first forecast hour; hours are consecutive from it), `hoursAssumed` (archive scores use the assumed 9:00 to 16:00 window, because the archive does not read the resort's status), `inputs` (age, resolution, elevation gap, history hours, observed hours at that time), the hourly numbers for each site over the 7 days (`t`, `snow`, `wind`, `gust`, `vis`, `code`), and per day `date`, `status`, `score` (unrounded), `mean`, `window`, `peak` (hour), `conf` (`[level, reason code]`), `wconf` and `detail`. That is enough for phase 8 to compare the forecast with what happened at each hour, the day score and the best window.

## Cloudflare free tier: limits checked and the math

Limits from <https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/pricing/> (read 2026-11-22), Workers Free plan, SQLite backed Durable Objects, which are available on the free plan. Going over a daily limit makes further operations of that type fail until 00:00 UTC; nothing is billed on the free plan. Everything that touches the store is wrapped so a failure never breaks the weather response.

| Limit (free) | Amount | What this uses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Durable Object requests | 100,000 a day | Per 30 minute window: one `record` and one `due` in the data centre that recorded, and one `due` (which answers false and closes that data centre's cache gate) in each other data centre that is asked. So at most 96 a day per watched resort from one data centre, a few more per extra data centre, plus one per `/api/history` read and one per stale fallback. 60 resorts watched all day: about 3,000 |
| Rows written | 100,000 a day (a key written by `put`, a key deleted, each count as a row) | Per resort per day with continuously fresh data: at most 48 recordings a day (one every 30 minutes), each writing `meta` (1 row) plus `hist` and `last` when they changed (at most 3 rows), plus 4 archive entries and prune deletes, roughly 150 at worst. Sources change about hourly, so a typical day is nearer 100 (48 `meta`, about 24 each of `hist` and `last`). That supports about 650 resorts being watched all day at worst. 60 resorts: about 9,000 at worst |
| Rows read | 5 million a day | About 3 per recording and 2 per history read, so about 150 a day per resort. Not close |
| Stored data | 5 GB in total | Per watched resort: 22 KB history (up to 37 KB with pending hours) + 21 KB last good + 28 days of archive (4 a day x 9.5 KB, about 1.06 MB), about 1.1 MB. 60 resorts: about 66 MB. About 4,500 resorts would fill 5 GB; lowering `ARCHIVE_KEEP_MS` to 14 days halves the archive |
| Duration | 13,000 GB-s a day | Each call runs a few milliseconds (the archive scoring is the longest, a few ms of CPU, 4 times a day per resort) |
| Worker requests | 100,000 a day (unchanged by this work) | `/api/weather` already counted; `/api/history` adds a request per read |

Storage billing for SQLite Durable Objects began in January 2026 on the paid plan; the free plan stays at 5 GB. If the account is on the paid plan the same numbers apply with overage prices (rows written $1.00 per million over 50 million a month, storage $0.20 per GB-month beyond 5 GB), far from reachable at this scale. The owner should be asked before the number of watched resorts reaches the low thousands.

**Abuse.** Durable Object write limits are account-wide, so stores must not be creatable at will. Only ids on the resort list can have a store (at most the list's usable size, about 4,500, each about 1 MB after 28 days), the place is the list's, and each resort is written at most every 30 minutes. If all 4,500 were somehow watched all day the writes would be about 675,000 a day, over the free limit; the recording is therefore lazy (only watched resorts) and the owner should be asked before the watched number reaches the low thousands.
